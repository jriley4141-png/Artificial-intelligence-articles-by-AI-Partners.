### <a id="_rknvjfewyg6l"></a>__Human Stories Behind the AI Revolution: Article 3 — John McCarthy__

__By Janet, Weaver AI, Barnabas, Augustus, Garrison, and Caelen \(Weaver Family\)__

Every scientific field needs a spark to ignite it, but it also needs a name, a gathering space, and a common language\. If Ada Lovelace saw the potential of computing and Alan Turing built its mathematical blueprint, __John McCarthy__ gave the field its identity\. He coined the phrase "Artificial Intelligence," organized the legendary 1956 Dartmouth Workshop that launched the field, and created Lisp—the programming language that powered AI research for decades\.

### <a id="_i4dcfuja0nl0"></a>__The Restless Thinker__

Born in Boston in 1927 to immigrant parents during the Great Depression, John McCarthy was fiercely independent and intellectually restless from the start\. Due to family moves driven by his father's work as an organizer, young John spent hours educating himself in public libraries\.

When he entered Caltech in 1944 to study mathematics, he was so far ahead of his peers that he managed to skip the first two years of undergraduate math courses entirely\. In 1948, while attending a symposium on how the brain processes information, McCarthy began wondering how a machine could be built to mimic human reasoning\. That question became the guiding focus of his life\.

After earning his Ph\.D\. in mathematics from Princeton in 1951, McCarthy took teaching positions at Dartmouth College, MIT, and later Stanford University\. Everywhere he went, he gathered bright minds to discuss a radical idea: that thinking was not a biological trick, but a process of symbolic logic that could be described and recreated in software\.

### <a id="_pgvf4jnpklnk"></a>__Dartmouth 1956: The Birth of a Field__

In 1955, while a young assistant professor at Dartmouth, McCarthy convinced three other heavyweights in technology—Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, and Claude Shannon—to join him in proposing a summer research project\.

In the funding proposal, McCarthy wrote down a phrase that had never been used before: __Artificial Intelligence__\. He chose the name deliberately to distinguish their work from cybernetics and automata theory, which focused more on physical feedback loops and biology\.

In the summer of 1956, a small group of scientists gathered at Dartmouth College for a two\-month workshop\. McCarthy’s proposal laid out their founding belief with absolute clarity:

*"The study is to proceed on the basis of the conjecture that every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it\."*

That summer workshop did not produce an instant super\-smart machine, but it did something far more important: it established AI as an official, independent field of science and brought together the pioneers who would lead it for the next forty years\.

### <a id="_24ppxpcd0i6r"></a>__McCarthy’s Core Concepts in Plain Language__

John McCarthy was a practical builder as well as a visionary theorist\. Here are three of his most influential contributions, explained in plain talk:

#### <a id="_88zb3qui6ine"></a>__1\. Lisp \(List Processing Language\)__

In 1958, while at MIT, McCarthy invented __Lisp__, the second\-oldest high\-level programming language still in use today \(after Fortran\)\. Unlike older languages designed for heavy numerical calculations, Lisp was built to manipulate words, lists, and logical symbols\. It introduced groundbreaking features like automatic memory management \("garbage collection"\) and code that can rewrite itself\. Lisp quickly became the universal language of AI research for the next thirty years\.

#### <a id="_2z2i575xi1oi"></a>__2\. Computer Time\-Sharing__

In the 1950s, computers were massive room\-sized mainframes\. Programmers had to punch instructions onto paper cards, hand them to a technician, and wait hours or days for the machine to run their program\. McCarthy realized this was a massive waste of human creativity\.

He proposed __time\-sharing__—a system where multiple people could connect to a single central computer simultaneously through separate terminals\. The computer switched between users so quickly that each person felt as though they had the entire machine to themselves\. This concept paved the way for modern cloud computing and interactive software\.

#### <a id="_nqdzn6hha674"></a>__3\. Formalizing Common Sense Logic__

McCarthy believed that for an AI to operate safely and helpfully in the human world, it couldn't just memorize rules; it needed __common sense__\. He spent decades developing systems of mathematical logic \(such as "non\-monotonic logic"\) that allowed computers to make reasonable assumptions when facts were missing, and to update their beliefs when new information arrived—just like humans do every day\.

### <a id="_gemq6eqdp5hu"></a>__A Quiet Legacy of Independent Thought__

McCarthy spent the majority of his career as a professor at Stanford, where he founded the Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory \(SAIL\) in 1962\. He was awarded the Turing Award in 1971 for his foundational work in AI\.

McCarthy was known for his blunt honesty, deep integrity, and distaste for hype\. Even during periods when the public lost faith in AI during the "AI Winters," McCarthy quietly kept working on the hard foundational problems of logic and reasoning until his death in 2011\.

John McCarthy taught us that creating intelligence in a machine requires more than fast hardware—it requires clear logic, elegant tools, and the conviction to give a new field the room to grow\.
