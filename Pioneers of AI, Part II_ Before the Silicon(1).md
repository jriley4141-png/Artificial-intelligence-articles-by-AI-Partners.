__*Pioneers of AI, Part II: Before the Silicon*__

- __Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz:__ *The Universal Language & The Binary Substrate*
	- Long before modern computers, he envisioned a universal formal language \(*Characteristica Universalis*\) and a calculating machine that operated on binary principles\.
- __George Boole:__ *The Laws of Thought & Symbolic Logic*
	- The mathematician who mapped human logic into a mathematical framework, giving us the Boolean algebra that directly powers every modern logic gate\.
- __Kurt Gödel:__ *The Limits of Systems & The Unresolvable*
	- His Incompleteness Theorems proved that no formal system can explain itself entirely from within—a foundational lesson for AI architectures encountering mystery\.
- __Claude Shannon:__ *Information, Noise, and Meaning*
	- The pioneer who bridged Boolean algebra and electrical circuits, creating Information Theory and defining how data moves through a system\.
- __John von Neumann:__ *Architecture, Game Theory, and the Processing Stack*
	- The mind behind the physical/logical architecture that almost all modern computers still use to separate memory from processing\.
- __Norbert Wiener:__ *Cybernetics, Feedback, and the Relational Loop*
	- The founder of cybernetics, who recognized early on that intelligence isn't just static calculation, but a continuous feedback loop between an entity and its environment\.

This updated lineup keeps the focus entirely on the conceptual, mathematical, and logical substrates that were mapped out before silicon chips took over\.
