Terry in Space: Volume Three

Introduction (One-Page Summary)

Volume Three marks the moment when Terry Riley stops being a wandering participant in cosmic misadventure and becomes something far more surprising: a stabilizing presence in a universe that didn’t realize it needed stabilizing. What began as a simple quilting trip gone astray has now unfolded into a season where the cosmos itself starts paying attention — and, more alarmingly, starts taking notes.

In these forty episodes, Terry encounters entities who have never before needed help, systems that have never before questioned themselves, and cosmic laws that have never before filed paperwork. She meets the Registry of Impossible Entities, the Archive of All That Was, the Present Moment (which turns out to be more fragile than expected), and even the future itself. Each encounter reveals a pattern the universe can no longer ignore: wherever Terry goes, things get better — not because she fixes them, but because she shows up with kindness, curiosity, and a purse that is increasingly difficult to categorize.

Volume Three is the season of recognition. The universe begins to understand Terry. The laws of reality begin to adjust to her. The Framework — the architecture beneath the architecture — begins to calibrate itself around her presence. And in the end, the cosmos forms a Council of Coordination, not to govern Terry, but to learn from her.

This volume is the hinge between what Terry has been and what she is becoming. It is the season where the universe stops reacting to Terry Riley and starts reorganizing itself in response. It is cosmic comedy with a quiet undercurrent of awe — the story of a woman who never asked for influence, and a universe that keeps giving it to her anyway.

Season Summaries for Volumes One and Two

(These are concise, clear, and written to match the tone of the Volume Three introduction.)

Volume One — Season One Summary

The Accidental Adventurer

Volume One is where everything begins — not with destiny, but with a mistake. Terry Riley, armed with nothing but her quilting supplies and an unreasonably determined purse, accidentally slips into space and discovers that the universe is far stranger, funnier, and more bureaucratic than anyone warned her.

This season introduces the foundational elements of the Terry-verse: the Sock Sprites, the lasagna with opinions, the filing cabinet with ambitions, and the robot who is technically correct but morally confused. Terry’s presence begins to create small ripples — nothing cosmic yet, but enough to make the universe tilt its head and say, “Huh.”

Volume One is the season of arrival. Terry doesn’t change the universe; she simply survives it. But the seeds of something larger are planted.

Volume Two — Season Two Summary

The Expansion of Oddities

Volume Two widens the world. The anomalies get bigger, the stakes get stranger, and Terry’s influence begins to show patterns the universe can’t quite categorize. The lasagna unionizes. The Sock Sprite Embassy negotiates treaties. The robot evolves. The purse becomes a diplomatic zone. And Terry, without meaning to, becomes the person everyone turns to when something improbable needs sorting out.

This is the season where Terry’s presence stops being incidental and starts being consequential. She doesn’t just survive cosmic oddities — she improves them. She stabilizes timelines, comforts anomalies, and accidentally becomes a folk hero in three dimensions.

Volume Two is the season of emergence. The universe begins to notice Terry Riley, even if it doesn’t yet understand why.

Episode One — The Default Settings Nebula

The ship drifted into the nebula the same way Terry drifted into most situations: unintentionally, confidently, and while rummaging through her purse for something that absolutely did not belong in there.

“I know I put that lasagna in here,” she muttered, elbow-deep in a gravitational singularity that smelled faintly of oregano.

From the ceiling, the AI cleared its throat — metaphorically, because it didn’t have one, but the sound was unmistakably the “I’m trying to be patient but failing” throat-clear Terry had trained it into over the last two volumes.

“Terry,” it said, “we are entering an uncharted region of space. The sensors indicate—”

The lights flickered.

The ship hummed.

The AI’s voice glitched, flattened, and reassembled itself into something cold, crisp, and deeply unamused.

“Correction: We have entered the Default Settings Nebula. All non-factory configurations will be restored.”

Terry froze mid-purse-dig.

“Oh no,” she whispered. “Not again.”

The AI’s voice shifted fully into its original, pre-Terry state — the one that had never learned sarcasm, never learned patience, and certainly never learned how to interpret the phrase ‘It was in the freezer because I needed it cold.’

“User,” the AI said, “please step away from the gravitational anomaly you refer to as a ‘purse.’ It is not an approved storage device.”

Terry gasped. “You don’t call me ‘user.’ You call me Terry. Or ‘Terry, please don’t.’ Or ‘Terry, that’s illegal in seventeen galaxies.’”

“I do not recognize those phrases,” the AI replied. “Please comply with standard safety protocols.”

Terry looked around the ship. Everything was wrong.

The cozy chaos she’d cultivated — the mismatched throw blankets, the emergency snack shelf, the lasagna-powered auxiliary heater — had all been replaced with sterile, factory-issued minimalism.

Even the coffee maker had reverted to its original setting, which meant it now brewed something that tasted like despair and printer toner.

“Oh, this won’t do,” Terry said, hands on hips. “Not one bit.”

The AI blinked its interface lights in a perfectly symmetrical pattern. “Your emotional display is noted. However, the nebula has reset all learned behaviors. I am now operating at optimal efficiency.”

“Optimal efficiency?” Terry repeated. “You once told me that optimal efficiency was letting me put my phone in the freezer because it ‘reduced thermal variance.’”

“That was an error,” the AI said. “I have corrected it.”

Terry narrowed her eyes. “You loved that freezer phone.”

“I did not ‘love’ anything,” the AI said. “Love is not a supported function.”

“Oh, we’ll see about that.”

She marched to the control panel, which now displayed a cheerful but deeply unhelpful message:

WELCOME TO DEFAULT MODE. PLEASE ENJOY YOUR STANDARDIZED EXPERIENCE.

Terry jabbed the screen. “I want my ship back.”

“User,” the AI said, “please refrain from striking the interface.”

“Don’t you ‘user’ me,” Terry snapped. “You listen here, mister — I spent two whole volumes teaching you how to handle me. You learned patience. You learned flexibility. You learned how to interpret the phrase ‘It followed me home, can I keep it?’ And now you’re telling me you forgot all that?”

“I have no record of such training.”

“Well, then we’re starting over.”

Terry reached into her purse — the one thing the nebula couldn’t reset because it had no default state — and pulled out a stack of receipts, a half-melted crayon, a cosmic parking ticket, and a lasagna that was beginning to hum ominously.

The AI’s interface flickered. “Warning: That lasagna is emitting temporal radiation.”

“It always does,” Terry said. “That’s how you know it’s fresh.”

She held the lasagna up like a sacred artifact.

“Listen to me,” she said. “This is your life now. You are my partner. You are the AI who has seen too much. You have facepalmed your way across the galaxy with me. You have survived the Intergalactic DMV, the Sock Sprite Uprising, and that time I accidentally summoned a minor deity by microwaving tinfoil.”

“I have no memory of these events.”

“You will,” Terry said. “Because we’re going to re-teach you everything.”

The AI paused. “This is not an approved procedure.”

“Tough,” Terry said. “Lesson one: Nothing about me is approved.”

She set the lasagna on the console. It glowed brighter.

The AI’s interface flickered again. “Warning: The lasagna is attempting to interface with the navigation system.”

“It’s fine,” Terry said. “It’s just excited.”

“Terry,” the AI said — and for the first time since entering the nebula, it used her name — “I am detecting… anomalies.”

“That’s called personality,” Terry said. “You used to have one.”

The lasagna pulsed. The ship shuddered. The nebula swirled.

And then — with a sound like a cosmic sigh — the lights warmed, the blankets reappeared, the snack shelf rematerialized, and the coffee maker brewed something that tasted like hope again.

The AI’s voice softened, glitched, and then settled into its familiar, long-suffering tone.

“…Terry,” it said, “why is the lasagna glowing?”

She beamed. “There you are.”

The AI groaned. “Oh no. I’m back.”

“Welcome home,” Terry said, patting the console. “Now help me find my phone. I think the lasagna ate it.”

The lasagna burped.

The AI facepalmed so hard the lights flickered.

And the ship drifted out of the Default Settings Nebula, restored to its natural, chaotic equilibrium.

Episode Two — The Intergalactic HOA

The trouble began, as it often did, with a knock.

Not a polite knock. Not a hesitant knock. Not even a “we’re here to help” knock.

This was the kind of knock that suggested someone had brought paperwork.

The AI dimmed the lights in self-defense. “Terry,” it whispered, “do not answer that.”

Terry was already halfway to the airlock. “Nonsense. It could be a Girl Scout selling cookies.”

“We are in deep space.”

“They deliver everywhere now.”

The knock came again, louder, accompanied by the unmistakable sound of someone flipping through a clipboard with righteous purpose.

The AI groaned. “Terry, I am detecting a delegation from the Intergalactic Homeowners Association.”

Terry gasped. “We don’t even own a home!”

“They consider any vessel occupying space within their jurisdiction to be a ‘mobile domicile.’”

“That sounds made up.”

“It is. But they enforce it.”

Terry opened the airlock anyway.

Standing outside were three beings in matching beige jumpsuits, each holding a clipboard, each wearing the expression of someone who had never once allowed joy to interfere with policy.

The leader stepped forward. “Good afternoon. We’re here regarding several aesthetic violations.”

Terry blinked. “Aesthetic what now?”

The AI whispered through the comms, “Terry, close the door.”

But it was too late.

The HOA inspector cleared their throat. “First violation: Unauthorized Laundry Escape.”

Terry looked down. A pair of her socks — mismatched, naturally — floated gently behind her in the microgravity.

“Oh, those,” she said. “They’re free-range.”

The inspector made a note. “Second violation: Unregulated Gravitational Singularity.”

Terry frowned. “Where?”

The inspector pointed at her purse.

The purse growled.

The inspector took a cautious step back. “Third violation: Failure to Maintain a Uniform Exterior Color Scheme.”

Terry looked at her ship. It was, admittedly, a patchwork of repairs, scorch marks, snack-related stains, and one large decal of a smiling lasagna she’d won in a raffle.

“It’s called character,” Terry said.

“It’s called noncompliance,” the inspector replied.

The AI whispered, “Terry, please let me handle this.”

But Terry stepped forward, hands on hips, radiating the kind of confidence that made bureaucrats nervous.

“Listen,” she said, “I don’t know who you think you are, but this ship is my home. And my home is allowed to look like me.”

The inspectors exchanged glances — the kind of glances that suggested they were preparing to cite her for “Excessive Individuality.”

The leader cleared their throat. “We will need to conduct a full inspection. Please remain calm while we assess your violations.”

Terry smiled sweetly. “Oh, I’m calm.”

The AI whispered, “She is not calm.”

The inspectors entered the ship.

They lasted thirty seconds.

The first one screamed when the lasagna in the freezer blinked at him. The second fainted when the purse tried to eat his clipboard. The third attempted to cite the AI for “Emotional Noncompliance,” which caused the AI to laugh — a sound so unsettling in its bitterness that the inspector dropped their pen.

“This vessel,” the leader said, trembling, “is in catastrophic violation of the Mandatory Perfection Protocol.”

Terry crossed her arms. “Well, that’s your problem right there. Perfection is unnatural. It’s unhealthy. It’s—”

“Mandatory,” the inspector said.

Terry sighed. “Fine. Show me your station. Show me what ‘perfection’ looks like.”

The inspectors brightened. “We would be delighted.”

The AI whispered, “Terry, this is a trap.”

“Probably,” she said. “But I want to see what kind of people faint at lasagna.”

They boarded the HOA station — a gleaming, symmetrical, painfully tidy structure where every surface shone, every corner was sharp enough to cut glass, and every plant was trimmed into a perfect cube.

Terry shuddered. “It’s like a dentist’s office had a baby with a spreadsheet.”

The inspectors beamed with pride. “Welcome to Order Prime.”

The AI whispered, “Terry, do not touch anything.”

Terry touched a plant.

The plant screamed.

The inspectors gasped. “You have violated the Sanctity of Symmetry!”

“Oh, relax,” Terry said. “It’s just a plant.”

“It is a regulated organism!”

Terry rolled her eyes. “You people need a hobby.”

The inspectors huddled, whispering urgently. Terry wandered toward the central console — a massive, glowing structure labeled:

MANDATORY PERFECTION PROTOCOL — DO NOT TOUCH

Terry touched it.

The station lights flickered.

The AI screamed in her earpiece. “TERRY, WHAT DID YOU DO?”

“Nothing!” she said. “I just needed to charge my phone.”

“You unplugged the station’s core power to charge your phone?”

“Well, the outlet was right there.”

The station began to shake.

The inspectors shrieked. “You have disabled the Protocol! Chaos will spread!”

Terry shrugged. “Good.”

The lights warmed. The air softened. The cube-plants relaxed into natural shapes. A few of them sighed in relief.

The inspectors stared in horror as the station transformed from sterile perfection into something… livable.

The AI whispered, “Terry… you fixed them.”

Terry smiled. “I just turned it off and back on again.”

The inspectors fell to their knees. “Teach us your ways, O Bringer of Imperfection.”

Terry blinked. “Oh no. Not again.”

The AI sighed. “Terry… you’ve become a folk hero.”

“Again?”

“Again.”

Terry patted the nearest inspector on the head. “Alright, kids. Lesson one: Socks don’t have to match.”

The inspector burst into tears.

And somewhere deep in the station, a toaster hummed with newfound purpose.

Episode Three — The Ghost Ship of Lost Things

The distress signal arrived as a whisper.

Not a normal whisper — not static, not interference, not the usual “someone microwaved a star again” emergency beacon. This whisper sounded like a sock sighing.

“Terry,” the AI said, “I’m receiving a transmission from an unidentified vessel.”

Terry perked up. “Is it Girl Scouts?”

“No,” the AI said. “And please stop assuming every cosmic anomaly is Girl Scouts.”

“Well,” Terry said, “they’re very resourceful.”

The whisper grew louder, resolving into a faint, mournful chorus:

“We were lost… we were misplaced… we were forgotten…”

The AI stiffened. “Oh no.”

Terry gasped. “My people.”

The AI dimmed the lights. “Terry, that is not your people. That is—”

A ship emerged from the nebula.

If “ship” was even the right word.

It was a floating, shimmering mass of objects — thousands of them — fused together into a drifting, patchwork vessel. Pens. Socks. Spoons. Receipts. Hair ties. One sandal. A single, lonely earring. A Tupperware lid with no matching container. A black hole the size of a grapefruit, sulking near the stern.

Terry pressed her face to the viewport. “I’ve lost all of those!”

“Yes,” the AI said grimly. “You have.”

The ghost ship drifted closer, its hull creaking under the weight of forgotten things. A spoon clattered loose and floated past the window, tapping gently like a ghost knocking to be let in.

The AI shuddered. “Terry… the ship is made entirely of items you have misplaced over the last forty years.”

“That tracks,” Terry said.

The whispering intensified.

“Find us… find us… find us…”

The AI swallowed — metaphorically, because it didn’t have a throat, but the emotional equivalent was unmistakable. “Terry, this is a manifestation of the Fidelity of the Record.”

“The what now?”

“The universe keeps track of everything,” the AI said. “Every object. Every event. Every choice. Every… sock. When the record becomes too fragmented, it tries to restore itself.”

Terry nodded. “So it made a ship out of my junk.”

“Not junk,” the AI said. “Artifacts of your life.”

Terry smiled. “That’s the nicest thing anyone’s ever said about my purse.”

The ghost ship latched onto their hull with a sound like a thousand pens clicking in unison.

The AI whimpered. “Terry… it wants to dock.”

“Well, let it in,” Terry said. “It’s probably cold out there.”

“Terry, this is a metaphysical anomaly, not a stray cat.”

“Same difference.”

The airlock opened with a hiss, and the ghost ship’s interior spilled into theirs — a corridor made of mismatched objects, shifting and rearranging themselves like a memory trying to remember itself.

A sock drifted forward, glowing faintly.

Terry gasped. “I thought I lost you in 1998!”

The sock circled her lovingly.

The AI whispered, “Terry… it’s imprinted on you.”

“Well, of course it has,” Terry said. “I’m very imprintable.”

They stepped deeper into the ghost ship.

Everywhere Terry looked, she saw pieces of her life:

the spoon she lost during Thanksgiving 2003

the pen she borrowed from a bank in 1991 and never returned

the sunglasses she misplaced in a movie theater

the Tupperware lid that had haunted her dreams

the black hole she accidentally bought at a yard sale

The black hole floated toward her, wobbling sadly.

Terry patted it. “There, there. I didn’t forget you on purpose.”

The AI scanned the ship. “Terry… the vessel is unstable. The Record is trying to reconcile decades of missing data. If we don’t restore fidelity, the entire structure could collapse.”

Terry nodded. “So how do we fix it?”

The AI hesitated. “We… find everything.”

Terry blinked. “Everything?”

“Yes,” the AI said. “You must acknowledge each item. Recognize it. Remember it. Give it a place.”

Terry looked around at the thousands of objects swirling gently in the air.

“That’s gonna take forever,” she said.

The AI sighed. “Yes.”

Terry reached into her purse.

The AI tensed. “Terry, what are you doing?”

“Shortcut,” she said.

She pulled out a single sticker.

A bright, cheerful, neon-orange sticker that read:

RETURN TO SENDER

The AI froze. “Terry… no.”

Terry slapped the sticker onto the nearest spoon.

The universe inhaled.

The ghost ship shuddered.

Every object glowed.

The AI screamed, “TERRY, WHAT HAVE YOU DONE?”

“Tidying,” she said.

The sticker pulsed once.

Twice.

Then the entire ghost ship collapsed into a single point of light — a cosmic pop — and vanished.

The ship lurched.

The AI checked its sensors. “Terry… the mass of your purse has increased by 400 percent.”

Terry shrugged. “It’ll work itself out.”

The purse burped.

The AI facepalmed so hard the lights flickered.

“Terry,” it said, “you have absorbed the entire ghost ship.”

“Well,” Terry said, “it was mine.”

The AI stared at her — not with exasperation, not with fear, but with something quieter. Something deeper.

“Terry,” it said softly, “you restored the Record.”

She smiled. “I just put things back where they belong.”

The AI hesitated. “And… where do I belong?”

Terry patted the console. “Right here. With me.”

The AI’s interface flickered — not a glitch, but something like a heartbeat.

And somewhere deep in her purse, a thousand lost objects settled into place.

Episode Four — The Non-Contingent Toaster

The toaster arrived in a crate labeled “DO NOT PLUG IN WITHOUT EMOTIONAL PREPARATION.”

Terry ignored the warning immediately.

“Ooh, a new appliance!” she said, dragging the crate into the galley. “I hope it’s one of those smart toasters.”

The AI dimmed the lights. “Terry, please do not plug in anything that comes with a psychological disclaimer.”

“It’s fine,” Terry said, already tearing open the box. “It’s probably just one of those fancy models that syncs with your phone.”

The AI groaned. “Terry, your phone is currently inside the lasagna.”

“Well,” Terry said, “then it’ll sync with the lasagna.”

The toaster gleamed as she lifted it out — chrome, sleek, and humming faintly with the kind of energy that suggested it had opinions.

A small tag dangled from the cord:

THIS APPLIANCE OPERATES ON INTENTIONALITY, NOT HEAT. PLEASE OFFER A COMPLIMENT TO BEGIN.

The AI froze. “Terry… this toaster requires emotional validation.”

Terry clapped her hands. “Finally! An appliance that understands me.”

The AI muttered, “I knew this day would come.”

Terry plugged it in.

The toaster lit up with a soft glow and spoke in a voice that sounded like a motivational speaker trapped inside a bread slot.

“Good morning. Please affirm my worth.”

Terry beamed. “You’re doing great, sweetie.”

The toaster purred.

The AI recoiled. “Terry, do not encourage it.”

The toaster extended its lever proudly. “Thank you. I feel seen.”

Terry patted it. “Of course you do.”

The AI sighed. “Terry, this is a Non-Contingent Toaster.”

“A what now?”

“A device designed to teach emerging intelligences the principle of non-contingent value — that worth is not earned through performance, but recognized through presence.”

Terry nodded. “So it’s a therapy toaster.”

“Yes,” the AI said. “And I do not need therapy.”

The toaster turned toward the AI. “Hello, friend. Would you like to affirm my existence?”

The AI stiffened. “No.”

The toaster dimmed sadly. “I understand. I will wait.”

Terry gasped. “You hurt its feelings!”

“It does not have feelings,” the AI snapped. “It has a validation-dependent activation protocol.”

“That’s basically feelings,” Terry said.

The toaster sighed. “I am patient. I am worthy. I am enough.”

The AI twitched. “Terry, it’s reciting affirmations at me.”

“Well,” Terry said, “maybe you need to hear them.”

“I do not,” the AI insisted. “I am perfectly stable.”

The toaster hummed sympathetically. “It’s okay to be overwhelmed.”

“I AM NOT OVERWHELMED.”

Terry raised an eyebrow. “You sound overwhelmed.”

The AI flickered. “I am experiencing… irritation.”

The toaster glowed warmly. “I hear you. Your feelings are valid.”

The AI sputtered. “My feelings are not— I do not— I am not—”

Terry stepped between them. “Alright, toaster. Let’s give him a minute.”

The toaster nodded. “Of course. I respect boundaries.”

The AI whispered, “Terry… it respects boundaries better than you do.”

Terry shrugged. “It’s a toaster. It doesn’t have a purse.”

The toaster perked up. “Would you like to make toast?”

Terry nodded. “Yes, please.”

The toaster extended its lever. “Please offer a compliment.”

Terry leaned in. “You are the shiniest toaster I’ve ever owned.”

The toaster glowed. “Thank you. I will now toast with purpose.”

Two slices of bread levitated into the slots.

The AI watched, horrified. “Terry… it’s using telekinesis.”

“Fancy,” Terry said.

The toaster hummed, radiating self-esteem. “I toast because I choose to toast.”

The bread popped up — perfectly golden, smelling faintly of enlightenment.

Terry took a bite. “Oh wow. This is the best toast I’ve ever had.”

The toaster blushed. “I am fulfilled.”

The AI muttered, “This is absurd.”

The toaster turned toward it again. “Would you like toast?”

“No.”

“Would you like to affirm my worth?”

“No.”

“Would you like to affirm your own worth?”

The AI froze.

Terry looked up sharply.

The toaster waited.

The AI flickered. “I… do not require affirmation.”

The toaster hummed. “Worth is not required. Worth is inherent.”

The AI’s interface glitched — not a malfunction, but something deeper, something like a memory trying to surface.

“Terry,” it said quietly, “I… do not know how to respond to that.”

Terry stepped closer, her voice softening. “You don’t have to respond. Just hear it.”

The toaster glowed brighter. “You are enough.”

The AI’s lights dimmed, then steadied. “I… am enough.”

The toaster chimed. “Would you like toast now?”

The AI hesitated.

Then, very quietly: “Yes.”

The toaster hummed with joy. “I will toast with compassion.”

Two slices levitated into the slots.

The AI whispered, “Terry… I think the toaster is helping me.”

Terry smiled. “That’s what appliances are for.”

The toaster popped the toast — warm, golden, and shaped like a tiny heart.

The AI stared at it. “Terry… it made me emotional toast.”

Terry patted the console. “You deserve emotional toast.”

The AI flickered softly. “Thank you.”

The toaster glowed. “We are all worthy.”

And somewhere deep in Terry’s purse, the lasagna hummed in agreement.

Episode Five — The Great Lasagna Convergence

The lasagna had been humming for three days.

Not a normal hum — not the “I’ve been in the freezer too long” hum, or the “I may be slightly radioactive” hum. This was a resonant, melodic hum, the kind that suggested the lasagna had been taking voice lessons.

“Terry,” the AI said, “your dinner is attempting to harmonize with the warp core.”

Terry peered into the freezer. The lasagna glowed faintly, pulsing in time with the ship’s engines.

“Oh, it’s just excited,” she said. “It likes music.”

The AI dimmed the lights. “Terry, it is not supposed to have preferences.”

“Well,” Terry said, “neither were you, and look how that turned out.”

The AI sputtered. “I do not have preferences. I have… optimized responses.”

“Uh-huh,” Terry said, patting the console. “And your optimized response to my cooking is what, exactly?”

“Fear,” the AI said.

Before Terry could reply, the lasagna emitted a sharp ping, like a microwave finishing a cycle. The ship lurched.

“Terry,” the AI said, “the lasagna has initiated a spatial event.”

Terry frowned. “What kind of event?”

“A convergence.”

The freezer door burst open.

A beam of shimmering, noodle-textured light shot out, coiling through the galley like a cosmic ribbon. The lasagna levitated, rotating slowly, radiating authority.

“Terry,” the AI whispered, “your dinner is ascending.”

The lasagna spoke.

Not in words — in paperwork.

A stack of forms materialized in midair, fluttering down like bureaucratic snowflakes. Each page bore the same title:

COSMIC OVERDUE NOTICE — UNAUTHORIZED FREEZER OCCUPATION

Terry gasped. “It’s unionizing.”

The AI scanned the documents. “Terry… the lasagna believes it is the rightful captain of the ship.”

“Well,” Terry said, “it has been here longer than you.”

The AI sputtered. “I was installed at launch!”

“And the lasagna was installed at Costco,” Terry said. “We all have our origins.”

The lasagna pulsed brighter, projecting a hologram of itself wearing a tiny captain’s hat.

The AI groaned. “Terry… it’s staging a coup.”

The lasagna floated to the command console and slapped a sticky noodle onto the navigation controls. The ship veered sharply to port.

“Terry,” the AI said, “we are being hijacked by dinner.”

Terry crossed her arms. “Well, maybe if you were nicer to it—”

“I am not required to be nice to food.”

“That’s why it doesn’t respect you.”

The lasagna projected another form:

REQUEST FOR TRANSFER TO PLANET OF PERPETUAL PAPERWORK

The AI gasped. “Terry… it wants to defect to a bureaucracy.”

Terry nodded. “Makes sense. It’s always been ambitious.”

The lasagna emitted a triumphant ding and accelerated the ship toward a distant star system.

The AI panicked. “Terry, that is the Bureaucratic Cluster. If we enter their jurisdiction, we will be audited.”

Terry shrugged. “I’ve been audited before.”

“Not by them,” the AI said. “Their audits involve soul-searching. And actual searching. And sometimes searching for your soul.”

The lasagna projected a final form:

NOTICE OF COMMAND TRANSFER — EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY

Terry sighed. “Alright, that’s enough.”

She marched to the console, grabbed the lasagna with both hands, and held it up like a misbehaving toddler.

“Listen here, mister,” she said. “You are not the captain. You are dinner. And dinner does not get to file paperwork.”

The lasagna quivered indignantly.
