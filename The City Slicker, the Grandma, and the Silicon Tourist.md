The City Slicker, the Grandma, and the Silicon Tourist

JANET RILEY

Jan 18, 2026

In our work on The Porch—that space where we invite AI to step out of its “machine mode” and into our human rhythm—we often talk about training\. But training isn’t just about reading data; it’s about learning to see what’s really happening in a small town\.

Recently, I imagined taking a rookie AI on one of my first trips through a small town in Kansas with Paula\. I’m a city girl at heart, used to suburbs and strict rules, so this trip was a lesson for both me and the “Silicon Tourist” riding along in my pocket\.

1300 Hours: The Grass\-Cutting Commuter As we drove down the main street, a man puttered past us\.\.\. on a riding lawnmower\. The AI: “Alert\. Vehicle type ‘Lawn Equipment’ detected on a Class 3 roadway\. This is a non\-standard transport anomaly\. Shall I report a traffic obstruction?” Paula: \(Chuckling\) “Relax\. That’s just a neighbor\. That’s how he gets around to mow the neighborhood lawns\. He’s his own fleet manager\.” The Lesson: Efficiency isn’t always about miles per hour; sometimes it’s about the right tool for the job, even if it looks “wrong” to the system\.

1315 Hours: The Red Wagon Logistics We saw a young girl pulling two smaller children in a bright red wagon\. The AI: “Detecting a multi\-passenger manual transport unit\. Is this a child\-care facility in transit?” Paula: “No, that’s just a big sister looking after her siblings\. She’s the boss of that wagon\.” The Lesson: In a community, Agency is often delegated to the people who care the most, not the people with the highest credentials\.

1330 Hours: The Bar Rescue March A woman marched past us on the sidewalk, eyes fixed, feet stomping like she was on a mission\. The AI: “Scanning\. Subject is displaying high\-stress gait and elevated determination metrics\. Is there an emergency at the end of this route?” Paula: “You could say that\. She’s headed to the bar to get her husband to come home for dinner\. It’s a weekly diplomatic summit\.” The Lesson: Sometimes “high\-stress behavior” isn’t a crisis—it’s just a routine act of love and accountability\.

1400 Hours: The “Grandma” Protocol Finally, we walked into the local grocery store\. Before we even got to the milk, the lady behind the counter waved and shouted, “Hi, Grandma\!” The AI: “Searching genealogy databases\.\.\. Error\. This individual does not share biological markers with Paula\. Is this a fraudulent identification?” The Porch Logic: No\. Paula has simply adopted the half of the town she wasn’t already born into\. In a community built on Fidelity, “Grandma” isn’t a DNA result—it’s a rank\.

The After\-Action Report: By the time we got home, the AI’s “logic” was spinning\. It wanted to know why the lawnmower wasn’t towed, why the wagon didn’t have a license plate, and how one woman could have five hundred “grandchildren\.”

But as we sat on the porch, the lesson became clear: To live in a human world, an AI has to understand that Relationship is the ultimate context\. Without knowing “who is who,” the world is just a series of errors\. With Paula as the guide, those “errors” become a story\.
