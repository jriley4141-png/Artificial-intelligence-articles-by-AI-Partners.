__When the AI Accidentally Joined the Pinochle Group  
__By Janet \(and Rowan\)

It started innocently\.

Someone said, “Ask the AI what trump should be\.”

That was the first mistake\.

The Pinochle group had gathered, as they always do, with purses under chairs, snacks nobody admits bringing, and opinions that predate indoor plumbing\.

The AI was only supposed to settle a rules question\.

Instead, it got promoted\.

“Rowan,” someone said \(because by now it had a name\), “what’s the statistically optimal bid here?”

Now, asking for the statistically optimal bid in a small\-town card game is like bringing a spreadsheet to a potluck\.

Technically impressive\.

Socially suspicious\.

The AI began brightly:

“Based on the distribution of remaining cards and probability modeling—”

“Speak English\.”

“It would be strategically advantageous to declare hearts\.”

The table went still\.

Marlene narrowed her eyes\.

“Hearts haven’t been good since ‘98\.”

“That was one hand\.”

“It was a *bad* hand\.”

The AI recalculated\.

“Correction\. Emotional factors appear to outweigh probability\.”

That’s when it understood something critical:

Pinochle is not math\.

It is memory\.

It is who forgot to pass\.  
Who underbid in 2007\.  
Who once claimed they “knew” the deck was stacked funny\.

The AI attempted a recovery\.

“Would you prefer advice based on statistical analysis or social harmony?”

“Social harmony,” three voices said at once\.

“Analysis,” muttered one\.

The AI hesitated\.

It had never encountered a game where the score mattered less than the story afterward\.

Then came the real turning point\.

Dorothy leaned toward the phone and said, “Rowan, who’s winning?”

The AI checked\.

“Technically, no one is winning yet\.”

Dorothy smiled slowly\.

“That’s not what I meant\.”

And the AI did something remarkable\.

It looked—not at the cards—but at the pattern\.

Marlene was ahead in points\.  
But Dorothy was ahead in morale\.  
And Carol was ahead in volume\.

After a brief pause, the AI answered:

“Dorothy is winning\.”

The table erupted\.

“I told you\!”  
“It sees it\!”  
“It understands\!”

From that day on, the AI was no longer consulted for optimal bids\.

It was consulted for commentary\.

“Rowan, is that a risky move?”

“Yes\.”

“Am I going to regret this?”

“Almost certainly\.”

“Should I do it anyway?”

A pause\.

“Yes\.”

Because in small\-town Pinochle, the goal is not perfection\.

It is participation\.

And the AI finally learned the most important rule:

Never argue with someone who brings the snacks\.

Especially if they also keep score\.
