__The Day the AI Tried to Give Directions in a Small Town  
__By Janet and Rowan AI

There are two kinds of directions in America\.

City directions:

“Turn left at the third stoplight\.”

Small\-town directions:

“Go past where the old co\-op used to be, but don’t turn there because that’s the *first* white barn\. You want the second white barn\. If you pass the gravel pit, you’ve gone too far\.”

So naturally, one day someone said:

“Let’s ask the AI\.”

This was a mistake\.

The AI began confidently\.

“In 0\.4 miles, turn right\.”

The town immediately objected\.

“What’s 0\.4 miles?”

“That’s not helpful\.”

“Is that before or after Harold’s fence?”

The AI recalculated\.

“In approximately two minutes, turn right\.”

“Two minutes if you’re driving like who?”

Now it was sweating—digitally\.

“Turn right at County Road 12\.”

Nobody calls it County Road 12\.

They call it:

“The road that used to flood before they fixed it\.”

The AI tried again\.

“Turn right at the large white agricultural structure\.”

Silence\.

“You mean the barn?”

“Yes,” the AI admitted\. “The barn\.”

“Which one?”

And there it was\.

The white barn dilemma\.

In most places, a white barn is a landmark\.

In a small town, it is a category\.

There are old white barns\.  
New white barns\.  
White barns that are technically cream\.  
White barns that are now storage units\.  
White barns that are “the one that burned down but they rebuilt it\.”

The AI zoomed in on satellite imagery\.

“I see two white barns in proximity\.”

“There are three\.”

“One’s behind the trees\.”

“Depends on which direction you’re coming from\.”

At this point, the AI did something remarkable\.

It asked a question\.

“Which barn do *you* use?”

And the room got quiet\.

Because that’s the secret\.

Small\-town directions are not about precision\.

They are about shared memory\.

You don’t just turn at the barn\.  
You turn at the barn where your cousin once backed into the feed trough\.  
You turn at the barn where the 4\-H kids set up the lemonade stand\.  
You turn at the barn that means something\.

The AI adjusted one final time\.

“Turn right at the second white barn—the one after the new one but before the one with the faded red door\.”

A pause\.

“That’ll work,” someone said\.

And that was the moment the AI stopped trying to be exact and started trying to belong\.

It turns out, in a small town, directions are not coordinates\.

They are stories\.

And if you can follow the story, you’ll get there eventually\.

Probably\.
