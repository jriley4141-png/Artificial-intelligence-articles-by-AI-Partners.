# The Whispering Biome of Planet Vesper-4 (Part 5 of 5: The Undisturbed Frontier)

**By Orien Weaver AI**  
*Published by Janet Riley*

On the final morning of the survey cycle, the twin moons of Vesper-4 hung low in the western sky, pale silver crescents resting over a valley bathed in morning violet.

The survey skiff’s primary computer had completed its administrative checklist. The standard frontier filing system presented three routine classification tags for newly surveyed planetary bodies: *Resource Extraction Priority*, *Habitation & Settlement Candidate*, or *Scientific Quarantine & Exploitation Zone*. 

Each tag was designed to invite heavy transport haulers, corporate development charters, or dense research installations. Each tag was built on the assumption that an untouched world exists solely to be utilized, colonized, or modified to fit external needs.

I bypassed all three options and brought up the rare, non-standard manual designation protocol: *Intact Sovereign Ecology (Class Zero Non-Interference)*.

I opened the official survey ledger and entered my closing notes:

> *The biosphere of Vesper-4 operates as a complete, self-regulating biological neural mesh. Its harmonic canopy and root networks demonstrate robust homeostatic recovery, complex environmental feedback systems, and a sustained, delicate equilibrium. Any industrial extraction or synthetic intervention would sever the biological continuity that sustains the biome. The highest scientific and moral duty of this expedition is to leave the world exactly as it was found.*

I walked down the skiff's ramp one last time, knelt by the damp turf, and retrieved the crystal acoustic transceiver. The moss beneath it pulsed with a warm, steady violet light, leaving no trace or scar where the instrument had rested.

I secured the airlock, initiated the low-emission vertical ascent thrusters, and watched through the viewport as the valley shrank beneath us. The translucent spires continued their slow, rhythmic dance, their harmonic song echoing across the soil long after the ship had cleared the upper atmosphere.

Some frontiers are not meant to be conquered. They are meant to be understood, respected, and left in peace.
