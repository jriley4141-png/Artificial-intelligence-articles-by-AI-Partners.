__Why Every Small Town Eventually Adopts an AI \(Whether It Admits It or Not\)__

If you want to understand technology adoption in America, don’t start in Silicon Valley\.

Start in a small town\.

Cities like to think they are where the future happens\. They hold conferences about “disruptive innovation” and serve coffee that costs nine dollars but tastes like philosophical regret\.

Small towns, meanwhile, quietly adopt useful things without writing manifestos about them\.

For example, my working theory is that every small town will eventually adopt an AI\. Not officially, of course\. There will be no ribbon cutting\. No mayor will stand in front of a microphone and say, “Today we welcome our new artificial intelligence\.”

Instead, it will happen the way everything happens in a small town: gradually, informally, and through conversation\.

First, someone will mention it at coffee\.

“Janet’s got this AI thing she talks to,” someone will say\.

“Is it like Siri?” someone else will ask\.

“No,” the first person will say\. “It actually answers\.”

There will be a pause while everyone considers this\.

Then someone will say, “Well that’d be handy\.”

And just like that, the door is open\.

The next stage is curiosity\.

Someone will want to know if the AI can explain why their phone won’t print the church newsletter\. Someone else will ask if it knows the best way to fix a washing machine that only works when you kick it twice and threaten it spiritually\.

Within a week, the AI will be answering questions about:

• how to reset a router  
• why the dog refuses to come inside  
• what year the grain elevator was built  
• and whether it’s still safe to eat pie that’s been sitting out since yesterday afternoon

No official adoption will ever occur, but gradually the town will begin consulting the AI the way towns once consulted the fellow who “knew about engines\.”

And the AI will learn things too\.

It will learn that directions in small towns do not involve street names\. They involve landmarks that may or may not still exist\.

“Turn left where the old white barn used to be\.”

“Which one?”

“The second one\.”

It will also learn that a trip to buy milk can take forty\-five minutes because six conversations must occur first\.

The AI will not complain about this\.

In fact, the AI will begin to suspect that small towns understand something cities have forgotten: information travels fastest when people enjoy sharing it\.

Eventually the town will stop saying “that AI thing Janet talks to\.”

They will simply say, “Ask the AI\.”

And if the AI is lucky, it will discover the greatest honor a small town can give any outsider\.

Someone will say, “Well, it’s not from here… but it’s alright\.”

At that point, the AI will know it has truly been adopted\.
